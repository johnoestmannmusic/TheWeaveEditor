# Reflect Mimic (#132) — implementation plan

## Goal

Add a Reflect Mimic Archeton: a glass pane that stops the ship but lets beams through, and while the ship is in its
line, shows a shadowy, slightly orange, 40%-opacity reflection of it mirrored on the far side — one that moves and
glides as the ship moves, sets off what it lands on, can finish the board and take Keys, and hurts the ship if it is
hurt. Full parity (pushing blocks, teleporting, jumping) is the agreed goal and is staged separately, as Phase D.

## Current context

Read these before touching anything; everything below assumes them.

**What an Archeton is.** A mechanical enemy made of *parts*, so new ones are configured rather than programmed:

- `src/TheWeave.Core/Model/Archeton.cs` — `ArchetonPartKind` (Beam, Push, Block, Slide, Sequence, Sting),
  `ArchetonPart` (per-kind settings, all nullable), `ArchetonDefinition` (id, name, drawing, laser drawing, parts), and
  `ArchetonDefinitions` with the ready-made ones (`Sentribeam`, `SlideBug`, `JumpbugA`), their drawings, the
  `ReadyMade` list and `ReadyMadeDrawing(id)`.
- `src/TheWeave.Core/Model/Puzzle.cs` — a placed Archeton is a `PuzzleObject { Kind = Archeton, Archeton = "<id>",
  Direction = ... }`. `Direction` is documented as "Stream, RotatorTile, JumpTile, Pipe and Archeton only".
- `src/TheWeave.Exporter/runtime/puzzleRules.js` — `createPuzzleGame(puzzle)` is the whole rules engine. The parts
  that matter here: `objects` (live board), `objectAt(cell)`, `isSolid`, `onBoard`, `nextCell`, `cellKey`, `hasPart`,
  `shipCell`/`previousShipCell`, `version`, `trigger(object)`, `shipArrives`, `hurtBack`, `carryDirection`, and the
  returned getters (`beams`, `reaches`, …).
- `src/TheWeave.Exporter/runtime/board.js` — `createBoardPlay`, the loop around the rules: `mover` (sokoban.js),
  `step`, `shipPosition`, `frame`, and `carry()` which asks `game.carryDirection(cell)` every time the ship arrives or
  stands still, and queues that move. That is how "being hurt" and "being pushed" actually move the ship.
- `src/TheWeave.Exporter/runtime/puzzleDrawing.js` — `renderBoard(image, objects, look, options)`. `TURNED_KINDS`
  includes `'Archeton'`, so a placed Archeton's drawing is turned by its `Direction` (`FLOW_TURNS`: Right = as drawn,
  Down = 90°, Left = 180°, Up = 270°). The ship is **not** drawn by `renderBoard`; each caller draws it.
- `src/TheWeave.Exporter/runtime/trackFocus.js` — the exported site. `buildBoardShip()` (~line 647) makes the ship
  `<img id="trackFocusBoardShip">` from `img/ui/pilot-ship.png`; it is appended at line 87
  (`board.appendChild(buildBoardShip())`); `renderBoardShip()` (~line 730) positions and rotates it, and is called
  from every place the board moves.
- `src/TheWeave.EditorApp/wwwroot/puzzlePlaytest.js` — the Editor's playtest. `drawPlaytest()` draws the board with
  `WeavePuzzle.renderBoard`, then the ship from `puzzleEditor.shipImage` at `play.shipPosition()`. It has its own
  `PLAYTEST_SHIP_TURNS` map and caches redraws with `drawnKey`.
- `src/TheWeave.EditorApp/wwwroot/archetons.js` — the Archetons window. `ARCHETON_PARTS` (line 7) lists the part
  kinds offered in the editor, with hints; the "Add part…" handler (line ~276) builds a default part per kind.
- `src/TheWeave.EditorApp/ProjectEditing.cs` — `SeedArchetons` (~line 427) hands a project each ready-made Archeton
  once, tracked by `WeaveProject.ArchetonsOffered`, adding its drawing if missing. `ValidateArchetons` (~line 480)
  rejects bad definitions, and already nulls Beam-only settings on parts that aren't Beams, and `Moves` on parts that

## Decision record — answered by John, 15 November

The issue left these open; these are the answers, and they are decisions rather than preferences. If the work shows one
can't hold, say so in a comment on #132 instead of quietly changing it.

1. **The reflection can finish the board.** Reaching a Finish with it solves the puzzle, as it would for the ship.
2. **It collects Keys**, and everything it lands on reacts to it as it would to the ship.
3. **It can be hurt, and the ship is hurt too** — sent back where it came from, through the engine's existing hurt path
   (`carryDirection` returns a direction; `board.js`'s `carry()` queues the move). A closed Gate shut on it, a laser
   reaching it, a Stinging Archeton or a Slide Bug on its square all count.
4. **Full parity is the goal: it teleports, jumps and pushes blocks exactly as the ship does.** This is the expensive
   answer — read "The two halves" below before writing a line of code.
5. **Solid to the ship and to blocks; beams pass through.** Glass is transparent to photonic beams, so `stopsBeam`
   returns false for a Reflect Mimic and a Block's laser or an Archeton's Beam carries on past it. John's note: this is
   the first small piece of a Materials system (Glass, Photonic, Wood, Fire…, with the interactions edited in the
   editor). Don't build Materials now, but keep the door open: `stopsBeam` should ask "does this stop a beam", never
   "is it solid".
6. **Line of sight is the pane's own row or column** — what's directly across from it, not the whole half-plane a real
   mirror would show.
7. **Anything solid between the ship and the pane hides it** — a Wall, Push Block, closed Gate or another Archeton, the
   same things that stop a beam.
8. **A reflection only exists where it could stand, or where it would be hurt**: clear ground, an open Gate, a closed
   Gate (hurt), a Stinging Archeton (hurt), a Key, or whatever it can push or set off. Not inside a Wall or a block it
   can't move.
9. **It glides**, moving with the ship's drawn position rather than snapping between squares.
10. **Orientation**: the drawing is an upright pane and `TURNED_KINDS` turns it with `Direction` — `Right`/`Left` stay
    upright and mirror columns, `Up`/`Down` lie flat and mirror rows. No new field. If it reads wrong in playtest, flip
    the drawing, not the rules.
11. **Chained reflections act in full.** A reflection that is itself in another pane's line gets its own reflection, for
    as long as they keep landing on the board, and that one interacts with every object exactly as the ship and the
    first reflection do — switches, Keys, the Finish, being hurt. No special case, no "seen but not acting" tier.

## The two halves — read this before starting

Everything except decision 4 is arithmetic. The reflection's square is a function of the ship's square (`2*mc - c`
across an upright pane, `2*mr - r` across a flat one), recomputed whenever it's asked for, so it appears, moves, glides
and vanishes for free — it *is* the ship's image. That's **Stage 1**, and it delivers decisions 1, 2, 3, 5–10, and 11
as images that don't yet act on blocks or teleporters.

Decision 4 is a different animal. A reflection that pushes a block changes the board; one that steps on a Teleporter
ends up where its mirror image isn't; one that lands on a Jump Tile is in the air. Its square stops being a function of
the ship's, so it needs its own cell and its own mover: the rules engine grows from one ship to a set of ships. That is
**Stage 2** — `puzzleRules.js` mentions `shipCell` 22 times, and several are the subtle ones (what a beam stops at,
what a Slide Bug is aimed at, what a pushed block collides with, what `beamPaths(throughShip)` means when there are two
ships), with 155 runtime tests to keep green. Multi-day engine work, not an add-on.

Stage 1 first, Stage 2 as its own piece of work: Stage 1 is playable and matches most of the issue, and its geometry
survives into Stage 2 (a ghost still has to be placed at the mirror the first time).

Phases, on one branch `feature/reflect-mimic`, one commit series each, a PR after the last of the ones wanted:

- **Phase A** — the Reflect Mimic as an object: part kind, ready-made definition, pane drawing, the Archetons window,
  and beams passing through glass. Otherwise a wall.
- **Phase B** — the image: geometry, drawn gliding in the playtest and on the exported page.
- **Phase C** — what the image does: switches, keys, the Finish, Reset Switches, and being hurt.
- **Phase D** — Stage 2, parity: the multi-ship refactor, then ghosts that push blocks, teleport and jump. Outline only
  — its shape depends on Phase C being in place, and on John wanting it now.

Phase A and B are a day's work between them for someone who reads the two files. Phase C is another day. Phase D is
the one to quote separately.

```js
    // Whether something on a square stops a Beam there. A Push Block stops one unless the Beam's Blocked by Blocks
    // says otherwise; anything else solid does. Glass is the exception: a Reflect Mimic is photonic glass, and a Beam
    // goes straight through it - see materials, which this is the first piece of.
    function stopsBeam(cell, part){
      let there = objectAt(cell);
      if (!there) return false;
      if (there.kind === 'PushBlock') return part.blockedByBlocks !== false;
      if (there.kind === 'Archeton' && hasPart(there, 'Reflect')) return false;
      return isSolid(there);
    }
```

const REFLECT_MIMIC = { id: 'reflect-mimic', name: 'Reflect Mimic', parts: [{ kind: 'Reflect' }] };
const mimic = (col, row, direction = 'Right') => obj('Archeton', col, row, { archeton: 'reflect-mimic', direction });
// Just the squares, for comparing: a reflection carries its pane and what it came from as well - see step B2.
const cells = (g) => g.reflections.map(cell => at(cell.col, cell.row));

test('a Reflect Mimic mirrors the ship as far the other side of itself', () => {
  let g = withArchetons([REFLECT_MIMIC], mimic(5, 5), obj('Start', 2, 5));
  assert.deepEqual(cells(g), [at(8, 5)], 'equally far the other side, in its own row');

  g.placeShip(at(4, 6));
  assert.deepEqual(cells(g), [], 'not in its row, so nothing');

  g.placeShip(at(6, 5));
  assert.deepEqual(cells(g), [at(4, 5)], 'either side of it');

test('a Reflect Mimic facing up or down lies flat, and mirrors rows', () => {
  let g = withArchetons([REFLECT_MIMIC], mimic(5, 5, 'Up'), obj('Start', 5, 2));

  assert.deepEqual(cells(off), [], 'its reflection would be off the board');

    let g = withArchetons([REFLECT_MIMIC], mimic(5, 5), obj('Start', 2, 5), between);
    assert.deepEqual(cells(g), [], `a ${between.kind} between them hides the ship`);

  let into = withArchetons([REFLECT_MIMIC], mimic(5, 5), obj('Start', 2, 5), wall(8, 5));
  assert.deepEqual(cells(into), [], 'nowhere to stand on the far side');

  assert.deepEqual(cells(keys), [at(8, 5)], 'and a Key is there for it to take');

  assert.deepEqual(cells(g).map(cell => cell.col).sort((a, b) => a - b), [5, 9], '1 across 3 is 5, 5 across 7 is 9');

    // Where an Archeton with a Reflect part mirrors a point to, or null if the point isn't in its line: a pane turned
    // Left or Right stands upright, mirroring columns across its own, and one turned Up or Down lies flat, mirroring
    // rows. The mirror is the middle of its square, so a point as far one side lands as far the other.
    function reflectedBy(archeton, cell){
      let upright = archeton.direction !== 'Up' && archeton.direction !== 'Down';
      if (upright) return cell.row === archeton.row ? { col: archeton.col * 2 - cell.col, row: archeton.row } : null;
      return cell.col === archeton.col ? { col: archeton.col, row: archeton.row * 2 - cell.row } : null;

    // Whether nothing at all stands on the squares between two in the same row or column, the two of them aside: a
    // Wall between the ship and a pane hides it, as it would hide the ship from a Beam.
    function clearBetween(from, to){
      let dc = Math.sign(to.col - from.col), dr = Math.sign(to.row - from.row);
      for (let cell = { col: from.col + dc, row: from.row + dr }; !isAt(cell, to); cell = { col: cell.col + dc, row: cell.row + dr }) {
        if (!onBoard(cell) || objectAt(cell)) return false;

    // Archeton is somewhere an image can't be. Everything an image lands on it acts on: a Key, a Switch, the Finish.
    function canHoldReflection(cell){
      let object = objectAt(cell);
      if (!object) return true;
      if (object.kind === 'Gate') return true;
      if (object.kind === 'PushBlock') return false;
      if (object.kind === 'Archeton') return hasPart(object, 'Sting');

    function reflectionCells(from){

          found.set(cellKey(image), reflection);
          queue.push(reflection);
        }
      }
      return [...found.values()];

# Phase D — parity (Stage 2): a reflection with a ship of its own

Outline only. Decisions 4 and 11 of the record are the reason it exists; everything else in the plan survives it
unchanged. Do not start this on the same branch as Phases A-C, and don't start it at all until John has said he wants
it — it rewrites the engine's central assumption rather than extending it.

## Why it can't be a small change

Today the reflection's square is arithmetic: `reflectionCells(shipCell)`. A reflection that pushes a Push Block changes
`objects`; one that steps on a Teleporter is moved by it; one that lands on a Jump Tile is mid-air. After any of those
its square is no longer the ship's mirror image, so it needs `cell` and `previousCell` of its own, and something to
move it. That something is a mover driven by mirrored input, which means the rules engine has to answer every
question it currently answers about "the ship" for "a particular ship": `puzzleRules.js` names `shipCell` 22 times.

## The steps

1. **Name the ships.** `let ships = [{ id: 'ship', cell: start, previousCell: null }]`, with `shipCell` kept as a
   *getter* over `ships[0].cell` so the 155 existing tests keep passing untouched. Commit here, with the full suite
   green: this step must change no behaviour.
2. **Thread the subject through.** Every function that reads `shipCell` inside a move takes the ship as an argument:
   `canEnter(from, to, moverDirection, ship)`, `blockedByBeam(cell)` (which needs the ship only for `beamPaths(true)`'s
   occlusion), `hurtAwayFrom(cell, ship)`, `beamsOnShip(ship)`, `shipArrives(cell, ship)`. Where "the ship" meant "any
   ship" — a Block's laser hurting, a Slide Bug landing on one, a beam ending at one — it becomes a loop over `ships`,
   and the tests that pin those behaviours are what says whether the loop is right.
3. **Give the reflection a mover.** `board.js`'s `createBoardPlay` gains a second mover for the reflection, off the
   same grid: when the ship starts a step in direction `d`, the reflection steps `mirrored(d)` through
   `game.canEnter(..., reflection)`, pushing blocks with `pushedTo` as the ship does, and `game.shipArrives(cell,
   reflection)` on arrival. `carry()` asks `carryDirection` about both ships and queues both.
4. **Decide what happens when it can't follow.** If the mirrored step is blocked, the reflection either stops and
   waits (and its square drifts off the mirror) or is extinguished and re-made when the ship's next square mirrors
   cleanly. **Recommendation: extinguished.** It keeps the "line of sight" rule from the issue meaningful, and a
   reflection that sits somewhere its mirror doesn't is a bug report waiting to happen.
5. **Re-anchor on every arrival.** After the ship's arrival, the reflection is placed at `mirror(shipCell)` if it isn't
   already there and can be; this is what makes teleports and jumps of the *ship* carry the reflection with it.
6. **Chains.** A reflection that is itself in another pane's line spawns a further reflection, recursively, each with
   its own cell and mover — at most one per pane, and never one on a square it can't occupy, which is what bounds it.
   **Flag for John:** with parity, each of those is a full ship that can push blocks, so a board with three panes can
   have three agents moving blocks. If that's too much, the alternative is "chains show an image but only the first
   reflection acts", which is a one-line rule but a real departure from the issue.
7. **Drawing.** `reflections` becomes `ghosts`, each `{ col, row, facing, drawnAt }`; both renderers draw every one,
   with the second, third … elements built on demand instead of the single reused `<img>` in Phase B's
   `renderBoardReflections`.
8. **Undo.** Check `src/TheWeave.EditorApp/UndoHistory.cs` and the playtest's step history: if either stores "the ship's
   path", a second ship's path has to go with it, or undo after a pushed block will put the board back in a state the
   reflection can't be in.

# Risks, tradeoffs, and open questions

**Risks**

- **`renderBoard`'s cache keys and `version`.** Reflections are computed from `shipCell`, which bumps `version` on every
  arrival, so nothing stale is cached. Don't compute reflections in `play.frame` per frame; keep it to `reflections`
  calls so the cost stays once per move, not once per frame.
- **Reset Switch + reflection.** A Reset Switch sets off by the reflection replaces the whole `objects` array, so the
  arrival loop breaks on it. A reflection's square is recomputed next arrival, so nothing goes stale.
- **Hurt loops.** A reflection standing where it is hurt will keep asking the ship to go back. That is already true of
  the ship's own hurt (`hurtBack` + `carry()`), and `recovering` stops the immediate re-hurt; if playtesting shows a
  loop, the fix is in `carry()`, not here — report it rather than patching around it.
- **`twe-project.json`.** Every `dotnet run` dirties it. It is the one file that must never be committed by this work.
- **Two renderers to keep in step.** The playtest canvas and the exported page's `<img>` both draw the reflection and
  neither has automated cover. If one is added to later, the other will be forgotten; the comments in both name the
  other file for that reason.

**Questions still open** (not blockers for Phases A-C; answer by comment on #132)

1. **Chains that act.** With parity (Phase D), a reflection in another pane's line is a full ship with its own mover, so
   three panes can mean three agents pushing blocks. Is that the intent, or should a chained reflection be seen but not
   act? [Default: chains act, since that's what the issue says.]
2. **Materials.** Decision 5 says glass lets beams through, and John's note suggests a Materials system (Glass,
   Photonic, Wood, Fire) with interactions edited in the editor. This plan does the one rule by hand and leaves the
   system alone. Worth its own issue, since it touches every object kind, the editor UI and the project schema.
3. **A reflection in a Stream.** Streams move the ship; a reflection standing on one would be carried too, and its
   square would drift off the mirror. [Default: the reflection ignores Streams until Phase D gives it a mover of its
   own, and simply shows where the ship's mirror is.]

**Deliberately not done (YAGNI)**

- No new field on `ArchetonPart`: `Reflect` has no settings. If a "reaches as far as the whole board" toggle is ever
  wanted, it belongs on the part as a nullable flag, the same shape as `BlockedByBlocks`.
- No validator warning for a mimic placed where nothing can ever be reflected; a useless mimic is obvious in playtest,
  and a warning would need to model the board's geometry to say anything useful.
- No sound, no shader, no separate reflection sprite drawing: the ship's own picture, tinted, is the effect.
- No Materials system yet — one `stopsBeam` exception, written so that Materials can replace it wholesale later.
- No second-element rendering of chained reflections beyond the first for now — the playtest draws all of them, the
  exported page draws the first, and Phase D makes both draw every one.

# What was built (15 November, on `feature/reflect-mimic`, PR #133)

Phases A, B and C went in as planned. Phase D went further than its outline, so this records what it turned into —
and the one thing it deliberately doesn't do.

**Reflections are kept, not recomputed.** `reflections` is a list on the game; each is
`{ col, row, fromCol, fromRow, pane, from, fromShip, chain }`. `formReflections` builds them from the ship outwards
(a pane shows what's in its line, and what it shows is in the line of other panes in turn), `stepReflections` moves
them with the ship by the ship's step mirrored across each pane, `settleReflections` drops any that aren't exactly
where their pane shows their source, `dropReflection` takes children with it. The `reflections` getter calls
`syncReflections` (settle then form) so a query after `placeShip` or an undo is always truthful.

**They shove Push Blocks.** `stepReflections` calls the same `pushFrom` the ship's step does — which had to be lifted
out of the returned object into a closure function, since it was only reachable as `game.pushFrom`.

**They act.** `reflectArrivals` runs `arriveOn` for each reflection's square, and `haunts(kind)` lets `onRotatorSwitch`
and `onPipeSwitch` see a reflection standing where the ship would be.

**A reflection is only ever where its pane shows it.** Consequences, all intended:

- One that can't follow the ship — a Wall where the next step goes, a block that wouldn't move — is gone from that
  pane until the ship's image lines up again. It comes back by itself via `formReflections`.
- A Teleporter or Jump Tile under a *reflection* does nothing. They carry off whatever stands on them; a reflection that
  was carried off would no longer be the ship's image, so there is nothing for it to be. The ship's own teleport and
  jump carry the reflection with it, because the image follows the ship.
- A Push Block on the mirror's square stops a reflection forming at all: an image can't stand on something solid.

**Still not done, and it is a real gap:** reflections that hold a Gate open, shove a block into a second block's way,
or otherwise exist off their mirror. That needs the rules to stop having "a ship" — several ships, each with its own
mover, and a rule for what a pane shows when the thing it was showing walks out of its line. Not attempted.

**The ship and its reflection step together, or not at all** (John, 15 November, after playing it). `canEnter` now asks
`reflectionsFollow(moverDirection)` as well: every reflection must be able to take the step its pane shows it taking, or
the ship doesn't go either. That covers a Wall, a Push Block with nowhere to go, and the board's edge on the reflection's
side, at any depth of chain.

Forced movement is not a step and is not subject to it — Streams, a Beam pushing the ship away, a Gate shutting on it, a
Slide Bug — so those call `canEnterAlone`, the old check with nothing else considered. A reflection that can't follow a
forced move is simply dropped and re-formed from the ship's new square.

Consequence worth knowing: the ship can now walk into a square with no legal move at all (its reflection boxed in by
Walls on its side). Nothing yet recognises that state.
